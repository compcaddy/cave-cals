# Trainer partnership: your unfair advantage

Most indie apps would pay thousands for what you have: a real personal trainer who'll give feedback, be on camera, and tell people about the app. MacroFactor grew on one trusted fitness voice (Jeff Nippard). This is that play at gym scale, and it can be copied to 50 trainers.

The trainer is referred to as **[Trainer]** throughout. Swap in their name.

---

## 1. What's in it for them (lead with this)

Trainers are busy and get pitched constantly. Make the deal obviously good for **their** business:

| They get | Why it matters to them |
| --- | --- |
| **Free Cave Cals+ for life**, plus free Cave Cals+ codes for their clients | Their clients get photo/voice logging, so compliance goes up and results go up |
| **Content that grows their own brand**: every video is posted on their account first, tagged as theirs | Free, professionally planned content. More followers means more clients. |
| **Revenue share**: their offer code `[TRAINER]` gets people 1 free month; they get **$10 per paid yearly subscriber and $2 per paid month** from their code (or 30% of first-year net; pick one) | Passive income from what they already say to clients |
| **Weekly Recap PDF from every client**, every Sunday | Saves them from "what did you eat this week?" conversations |
| **Title: "Cave Cals Head Coach"**, credited in the app's About screen if you like | Credibility, and something to put in their bio |
| **A say in the product**: monthly feedback session, their feature requests prioritized | People love building things |

**The ask of them:**
- One 2-hour filming session every other week (12 videos per session)
- Recommend Cave Cals to their clients and use it in check-ins
- One flyer at their gym's front desk, if the gym allows
- 30 minutes a month of product feedback
- Disclose the partnership on posts (the platform toggle or #ad). **Required even if they're only paid in free subscriptions.**

## 2. The pitch conversation (15 minutes, at the gym)

> "You know how clients say they'll track and then don't? I built Cave Cals to fix that. It learns their usual meals, so logging is one tap, and they can snap a photo or just say what they ate.
>
> Here's what I'm offering: free premium for you and your clients, a code that pays you $10 for every yearly subscriber, and I'll plan and edit short videos for your account. They grow your following as much as the app. And every Sunday your clients can send you a one-page recap of their week.
>
> What I'd need: a couple hours every other week to film, and your honest feedback. If something's wrong with the app, I want to hear it first."

Then **show it on their phone.** Install, run onboarding together, and log what they ate for breakfast. Watch them use Quick Add the next day.

## 3. Filming day: 12 videos in 2 hours

**Before the shoot:** pick 12 scripts from [03-Video-Scripts, Series A](03-Video-Scripts.md#series-a-your-trainer-12-scripts), print the beats on index cards (not word-for-word scripts), charge phone and mic, and set up a staged demo diary on a spare phone (or your own).

| Time | What | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Set up, check light and audio | Window light or ring light; clip-on mic |
| 0:10–0:25 | **B-roll bank**: lifting, racking weights, walking in, filling a water bottle, meal prep, holding phone and tapping, typing, snapping food | Used in every video. Film 20 clips of 3–5s each. |
| 0:25–1:25 | **Talking-head scripts A1–A8** | 3 takes each max. The second take is usually best. |
| 1:25–1:45 | **App-in-hand shots**: over-the-shoulder Quick Add taps, barcode scan, snapping a meal, "Hey Siri, log food" | Use the demo phone. Screen must be readable. |
| 1:45–2:00 | **Zog crossover** (B10) and 2 trend/stitch videos | Fun ending, keeps energy up |

**Editing:** CapCut (free). Auto-captions on, big text hook in the first frame, cut all pauses. 15–30s each. Post from the trainer's account and collaborate/cross-post to @cavecals (Instagram Collab posts put one video on both accounts, doubling reach for free).

## 4. Consent and release (do this before the first shoot)

A simple one-page release, signed:
- Permission to use their name, image, and voice in organic and paid posts for [12 months], renewable
- Compensation terms (free subscription, revenue share)
- They'll disclose the relationship on their posts
- **No client appears on camera, and no client data or numbers are shown**, without that client's separate written consent. Use a staged demo diary for all app footage.
- Either side can end the arrangement with 14 days' notice; published posts can stay up

## 5. Gym activation

### Front-desk flyer
Printable flyer: [assets/gym-flyer.pdf](assets/gym-flyer.pdf) (HTML source in `assets/`). It has a QR code to the App Store and a space for the trainer's code. Ask the gym owner first. Offer the gym its own code (`CAVE[GYM]`) and the same revenue share; many small gyms will say yes.

### "30-Day Cave Challenge" (run it in January, test it in November)
- **Sign-up:** a sheet at the front desk, or scan the QR code
- **Rules:** log food at least 5 days a week and weigh in once a week (optional), for 30 days
- **Check-ins:** each Sunday, participants text or email the trainer their Weekly Recap PDF (Progress → print icon → share)
- **Prizes:** based on **consistency** (days logged), **not** pounds lost. It's safer, fairer, and doesn't reward crash dieting. Prizes: a free PT session, a gym tee, a year of Cave Cals+.
- **Wall of fame:** first names and "days logged" on a whiteboard. Never weights.
- **Content:** the trainer films day 1, day 15, and day 30 group moments, with consent

### "Tracking 101" free workshop (30 minutes, in the gym)
1. Why tracking works (trainer, 5 min)
2. Everyone installs Cave Cals and sets a goal (10 min)
3. Log today's breakfast three ways: Quick Add, barcode, snap (10 min)
4. Q&A plus the 30-Day Challenge sign-up (5 min)

Film it (with attendees' consent). The group-energy footage is great content.

## 6. Client workflow (makes the app sticky for their whole roster)

1. New client intake: "Install Cave Cals, use code [TRAINER]." The trainer walks them through onboarding in session (5 minutes).
2. Set protein and calorie goals together in **You → Daily calorie goal / macros**.
3. Every Sunday: the client sends the **Weekly Recap PDF** by text or email.
4. The trainer reviews the averages, not daily noise. That's exactly how the recap is designed.
5. Monthly: the trainer and client look at Progress → Month together.

That's a real reason for the client to pay for Cave Cals+ (faster logging) and a reason to keep using it (their trainer checks in).

## 7. Scale to more trainers: "Cave Cals for Coaches"

Once the first trainer's workflow is proven (4–6 weeks), recruit more:

- **Who:** independent PTs and small gym owners, online coaches with 1k–20k followers
- **Offer:** the same as above: free lifetime Cave Cals+, codes for clients, revenue share, feature in @cavecals videos
- **Outreach DM:**
  > Hey [name], I'm Phil, the developer of Cave Cals (iPhone calorie tracker). [Trainer] at [gym] uses it with clients: they send a one-page weekly recap every Sunday instead of screenshots. I'm giving coaches free lifetime premium plus $10 per yearly subscriber from their code. Want me to set you up?
- **Landing page (later):** cavecals.vercel.app/coaches with a sign-up form, sample Weekly Recap PDF, and a short trainer testimonial video

Twenty trainers each bringing 10 paying clients a year is 200 subscribers. More importantly, trainers make content, so it compounds.

## 8. Monthly feedback session (turn the trainer into your product advisor)

Ask the same questions every month and write the answers in `First-use-feedback.md`:
1. What did clients complain about in the app this month?
2. Which feature did you actually show a client?
3. What did a client try to do and couldn't?
4. What would make you recommend it to *every* client?
5. What do other apps your clients use do better?
6. Which of our videos did people mention to you in person?

## 9. The trainer's first 8 weeks of posts

One theme per week, 4–5 posts a week on their account (cross-posted to @cavecals as Instagram Collab posts). Film two weeks at a time. Script IDs refer to [03](03-Video-Scripts.md) and the [cue cards](assets/trainer-cue-cards.pdf).

| Week | Theme | Posts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Why tracking** | A1 (#1 reason), A5 (3-week deal), carousel "How my clients send me their week", 1 Q&A reply video |
| 2 | **Protein** | A2 (how much), A7 (fake protein foods), slideshow "High-protein snacks under 200" (trainer checks the numbers), B5 Zog macros crossover |
| 3 | **The scale** | A4 (overnight gain), A11 (weigh-in routine), Weekly Recap walk-through, stitch a scale-panic video |
| 4 | **Real life** | A3 (weekend eating), A10 (eating out), A9 (Hey Siri), "what I ate today" with the app |
| 5 | **Myths** | A8 (cardio to burn off dinner), 3 stitches of viral diet myths, "Trainer ranks calorie apps" (A12, disclosed) |
| 6 | **Client stories** | Only with written consent: one client's 4-week consistency story (days logged, not pounds), or a staged example clearly labeled |
| 7 | **Challenge** | Announce the 30-Day Cave Challenge, sign-up sheet at the gym, day-1 group video |
| 8 | **Recap** | What 8 weeks of posting taught them, their favorite app feature, and the code in bio |

After week 8, repeat the formats whose videos beat the account's median views, and drop the rest.
