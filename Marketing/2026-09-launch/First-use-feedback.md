# A small first-use feedback session

Use this before spending on acquisition. The goal is to find confusing steps, not prove weight-loss outcomes or estimate a conversion rate from a handful of people. No sessions have been scheduled.

## Who and how

Invite about five consenting adult volunteers who have tried tracking food before. Prefer a mix of people who like detailed tracking and people who find it tedious. Ask for 10–15 minutes, and make clear that you want criticism of the app, not praise.

Use a test install or TestFlight build, with synthetic answers rather than their body details or health history. Do not record their face, voice, or screen unless they explicitly agree. For a quick session, handwritten notes about controls and hesitation are enough.

## Moderator introduction

“I'm testing whether Cave Cals is easy to understand. I'm testing the app, not you. Please say what you're looking for as you go. I'll mostly stay quiet so I can see where the app isn't clear. You don't need to share any real weight or health details.”

## Tasks

| Task | Observe without coaching | What counts as success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Open the app and get ready to log food in whichever way makes sense.” | Do they notice they can skip or set their own goal? Do the questions feel necessary? | They reach the home screen through a route they chose. |
| If they want the planner, give the synthetic card: male reference, 35 years, 180 cm, 90 kg, light activity, goal 80 kg, 0.5 kg/week. | Can they find units, enter values, go back, and explain the final target? | They recognize 2,050 as an editable estimate, not a promise or a recommendation for themselves. |
| “Log a snack with 200 calories.” | Where do they first tap? Can they find the confirmation? | A single correct entry appears in Logged. |
| “You meant 250 calories. Correct it.” | Do row taps and pencil affordances make sense? | The same entry is corrected, without an accidental duplicate. |
| “You want to change your plan, but decide to keep the old one.” | Can they find You, begin an update, and cancel? | The accepted target remains unchanged. |
| “You only want to track food, without weighing yourself.” | Do they understand the weight-tracking switch? | They can find the preference and explain what turns off. |

Do not force every participant through every onboarding branch. Use a fresh test install for a second route only if the participant agrees and time permits. Avoid deleting their real diary to reset the test.

## Questions after the tasks

1. What did you expect the app to do that it didn't?
2. What could we remove without making it less useful?
3. What do you think the calorie target means?
4. Which part would make you hesitate to use it tomorrow?
5. Which of the three campaign concepts best matches what you just used, and why?

Ask about ad concepts last so their words don't teach the participant how to use the app. Do not ask leading questions such as “Was that easier than Cal AI?”

## How to act on it

Fix a blocked task, accidental duplicate, misleading target interpretation, or lost change first. Treat repeated hesitation as a clue for a small copy/layout revision. Keep individual preferences separate from defects. Then repeat the affected task with fresh participants if practical.

Use this feedback to choose the first demo and first screenshot. Downloads, paid conversions, and retention require separate evidence; five successful sessions do not establish those results.
